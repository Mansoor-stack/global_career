## 0. Ground rules you must not get wrong

| Rule | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **IELTS General Training only** | IRCC does not accept IELTS Academic for Express Entry, however high the score. Select "General Training" at booking — double-check before paying. |
| **One Skill Retake is NOT accepted** | If one skill falls short, you must resit the full test (₹19,000), not a single-skill retake. |
| **Valid for 2 years from the test date** | Results must still be valid when you create the Express Entry profile *and* when you submit your PR application after an ITA. Expired results = refused application. |
| **Each skill converts separately** | No averaging. Your overall band is irrelevant to IRCC. |
| **In India, only IDP delivers IELTS** | The British Council stopped delivering IELTS in India in July 2021. There is no "British Council vs IDP" choice in Chennai — every booking goes through IDP. |
| **Paper IELTS is gone in India** | You choose between *IELTS on Computer* and *IELTS on Computer (Writing on Paper)* at selected centres. Speaking is still face-to-face with an examiner. |

### Your targets (unchanged)

| Skill | CLB 10 target | CLB 9 fallback | ≈ Raw score needed (out of 40) for target | ≈ Raw for fallback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Listening | **8.5** | 8.0 | ~37 (max 3 wrong) | ~35 (max 5 wrong) |
| Reading (GT) | **8.0** | 7.0 | ~37 (max 3 wrong) | ~34 (max 6 wrong) |
| Writing | **7.5** | 7.0 | — (examiner-marked) | — |
| Speaking | **7.5** | 7.0 | — (examiner-marked) | — |

Raw-to-band conversions are approximate; each Cambridge book prints its own table and it varies slightly by test. The key insight: **GT Reading is scored more harshly than Academic** (30/40 is only band 6.0 in GT), so CLB 10 in Reading means near-perfect Sections 1 and 2.

## 1. Why CLB 10 vs CLB 9 matters (CRS maths, verified Nov 2026)

### First official language points per skill (CRS core)

| CLB per skill | IELTS GT (L / R / W / S) | Points per skill – single | Points per skill – with accompanying spouse |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLB 7 | 6.0 / 6.0 / 6.0 / 6.0 | 17 | 16 |
| CLB 8 | 7.5 / 6.5 / 6.5 / 6.5 | 23 | 22 |
| CLB 9 | 8.0 / 7.0 / 7.0 / 7.0 | 31 | 29 |
| CLB 10+ | 8.5 / 8.0 / 7.5 / 7.5 | 34 | 32 |

### What the jump actually buys

| Move | Core points gained (all 4 skills) | Skill transferability effect |
|---|---|---|
| CLB 7 → CLB 9 | +56 single / +52 with spouse | **Unlocks the top tier** of both education+language and foreign-work+language combinations (up to +50 more, capped at 100 total) |
| CLB 9 → CLB 10 | **+12** (single or with spouse) | **None** — transferability already maxes at CLB 9 |

So be clear-eyed: **CLB 9 is the big jump; CLB 10 is a +12 point tie-breaker.** Each skill also counts on its own — e.g., getting Listening 8.5 with the rest at CLB 9 still adds +3.

### Why +12 is still worth chasing in your situation

1. **The 3 November 2026 CRS change** added a Labour Market Integration factor that rewards *Canadian* work experience or high-wage job offers. An applicant applying from India with no Canadian experience gains nothing from it, while many people in the pool gain points — so the general cut-off is likely to drift up for profiles like yours.
2. **Fewer federal spaces**: the 2027–2029 Levels Plan (5 Nov 2026) cut Federal High Skilled admissions (FSW + CEC + FST) to 100,000 a year from the 111,000 previously planned.
3. In a tight pool, 12 points is often the gap between an ITA and waiting another year. Language is the cheapest lever you control.

### Household note (important for your planning)

If your wife ends up as the **principal applicant** (e.g., for a healthcare-category draw), your IELTS counts only under *spouse factors*: max 5 points per skill, reached at **CLB 9**. In that scenario CLB 10 gives you **zero** extra points — CLB 9 is the real target. Decide who is principal (with a licensed RCIC, using both CRS scores) before over-investing in the last half-band. Also note: the FSW 67-point eligibility grid tops out at CLB 9 for language.

> PTE Core and CELPIP-General are also accepted by IRCC with identical CRS value. This plan assumes IELTS GT as you specified.

## 2. Diagnostic (Week 1)

### Which tests to take

| Order | Test | Why | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **British Council IELTS Ready – Member (free account)**: 50-min AI-marked Mini Mock + full mock tests, choose **General Training** | Fast first estimate + you see the computer-style format | Free |
| 2 | **Cambridge IELTS 18 General Training – Test 1** (full Listening, Reading, Writing in one 2 h 45 m sitting) | Authentic past paper with an official raw-score table — your *real* baseline | Book cost |
| 3 | **Speaking – Cambridge 18 GT Test 1 Speaking questions**, recorded on your phone | Baseline for fluency, grammar, pronunciation | Free |

Note: IELTS Ready **Premium** (40 scored tests) is unlocked only by booking with the British Council, which is impossible in India — don't plan around it.

### How to sit and score it

- **Listening**: play once only. Type or write answers *as you listen*. Give yourself only **2 minutes** at the end (computer-delivered has no 10-minute transfer time). Score with the answer key; convert with the book's table.
- **Reading**: 60 minutes strict, no extra transfer time. Score and convert.
- **Writing**: Task 1 letter (20 min) + Task 2 essay (40 min). Score yourself against the **public IELTS Writing band descriptors** (ielts.org) on the four criteria: Task Achievement/Response, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy. Self-scoring is usually 0.5–1.0 band too generous — get one AI estimate as a second opinion and assume the lower of the two.
- **Speaking**: transcribe your recording (phone auto-transcribe is fine). Count: long pauses, self-corrections, grammar errors per minute, and whether you answered with reasons/examples.

### Decide 8, 12 or 16 weeks

| Diagnostic result | Plan length | Realistic test window |
|---|---|---|
| L ≥ 34, R ≥ 34, W and S both ≈ 7.0 | **8 weeks** | Early–mid January 2027 |
| L 30–33, R 30–33, W/S ≈ 6.5 | **12 weeks** (this document) | Mid-February 2027 |
| L < 30 or R < 30, or W/S ≤ 6.0 | **16 weeks** (repeat Weeks 4–7 once) | Mid-March 2027 |

Honest constraint: 1 hour × 6 days = ~72 hours over 12 weeks. That is enough to add roughly 0.5–1.0 band per skill *if* most of it is active practice with error review, not video watching. If the diagnostic shows a gap bigger than 1 band in any skill, go straight to the 16-week version — it still fits your March 2027 target.

## 4. Skill-wise strategy for CLB 10

### Listening — target 8.5 (≈37/40)

**Computer-delivered specifics**: you type answers while listening; only **2 minutes** to review at the end; no spell-check. Both British and American spelling are accepted — the real killer is *misspelling* and plural/singular errors.

**Section 3 (academic discussion, 2–4 speakers)**
- Before audio: underline who is speaking about what; for multiple choice, paraphrase each option in your head.
- Track *agreement*: the answer is often what the two speakers finally agree on, not the first idea mentioned.
- Matching questions: the options appear in random order but the questions follow the audio order.

**Section 4 (lecture monologue, no mid-break)**
- Read all 10 questions in the preview time; predict word type (noun? number? plural?).
- Use the headings in the notes as signposts — if you hear the next heading, you've missed a gap; move on.
- Most CLB 10 candidates lose marks here on spelling of academic nouns.

**Distractor traps to drill**
- Self-correction: "the 14th — no, sorry, the 15th"
- Time shift: "originally… but now / we've changed that to…"
- Rejection: options listed, then dismissed ("we considered X, but…")
- Paraphrase mismatch: the option words are spoken but with the opposite meaning
- Word limit: "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" — three words = wrong
- Numbers: fifteen/fifty, 0 said as "oh", double letters ("double-L")

**Transcript review method (do for every practice section)**
1. Do the section timed, audio once.
2. Mark it. For each wrong answer, replay that 30 seconds **with the transcript**.
3. Highlight the exact words that gave the answer and the question words they paraphrase.
4. Log the error type: *spelling / distractor / lost place / vocabulary / word limit / plural*.
5. Add misspelled words to Anki.
6. Shadow 2 minutes of the Section 4 audio (speak along with the transcript) to train processing speed.

### Reading GT — target 8.0 (≈37/40)

| Section | Content | Suggested time | Realistic error budget for 8.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Section 1 | 2–3 short everyday texts (ads, notices, timetables) | 13 min | 0 |
| Section 2 | 2 workplace texts (policies, job info, training) | 17 min | 0–1 |
| Section 3 | 1 long general-interest text (hardest) | 27 min | 2 |
| Final check | Blanks, spelling, word limits | 3 min | — |

No extra transfer time on computer. Use the highlight tool — practise it in the familiarisation test.

**True / False / Not Given**
1. Find the *claim* in the statement and its qualifiers (all, some, only, always, more than, first).
2. Locate using names, numbers, capitalised words — answers come in text order.
3. **True** = text says the same thing in other words. **False** = text says the opposite. **Not Given** = text doesn't say it, or says something only partly related.
4. Never use your own knowledge. If you're debating between False and Not Given, ask: "Can I point to a sentence that *contradicts* it?" If not → Not Given.

**Matching Headings (usually Section 3)**
- Read the paragraph first, then the headings — not the other way round.
- Focus on the *main idea*, usually in the first 1–2 sentences, sometimes revealed by a contrast word ("however") mid-paragraph.
- A heading that repeats a word from the paragraph is often a trap.
- Do the easiest paragraphs first, eliminate used headings.

**Matching Information ("Which paragraph contains…")**
- Not in order; paragraphs can be used more than once.
- Identify what *type* of information is asked (an example, a reason, a comparison, a number, a warning).
- Do these **last** in a section — by then you know the text's layout.

### Writing GT — target 7.5

Timing: Task 1 letter ≥150 words in ~20 min; Task 2 essay ≥250 words in ~40 min. Task 2 counts double. Consider doing **Task 2 first** so it never gets rushed. The computer shows a live word count; aim for 170–190 (letter) and 270–300 (essay).

#### Task 1 — Letter

| Tone | Who | Open | Close | Style |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Formal** | Company, council, unnamed manager, editor | Dear Sir or Madam, | Yours faithfully, + full name | No contractions, polite and direct |
| **Semi-formal** | Someone you know but respect: landlord, manager by name, neighbour, teacher | Dear Mr Raman, / Dear Ms Lee, | Yours sincerely, / Kind regards, + full name | Polite, some warmth, few contractions |
| **Informal** | Friend, relative | Dear Priya, / Hi Arun, | Best wishes, / Take care, + first name | Contractions, phrasal verbs, personal tone |

**Structure for 7.5+**
1. Greeting matching tone.
2. Opening (1–2 sentences): state the purpose immediately.
3. Paragraph for bullet 1 — developed with specifics (dates, numbers, names, reasons).
4. Paragraph for bullet 2 — same.
5. Paragraph for bullet 3 — same.
6. Closing line asking for the next action / expressing expectation.
7. Sign-off + name.

**How to cover all 3 bullets properly**: before writing, jot 2 specific details per bullet. A bullet answered in one vague sentence caps Task Achievement around 6. Make up realistic details — the examiner cares about relevance and clarity, not truth.

**10 letter types to practise**
1. Complaint (product/service/neighbour noise)
2. Request for information
3. Apology (missed meeting, damaged item)
4. Invitation
5. Thank-you
6. Job application / enquiry about a position
7. Explaining a problem to a landlord or council
8. Asking to change an arrangement (booking, course date, work schedule)
9. Recommendation/advice to a friend
10. Accepting or declining an invitation/offer

#### Task 2 — Essay

**Band 7.5 structure (4 paragraphs, ~280 words)**
1. **Introduction** (2 sentences): paraphrase the question + clear position/answer.
2. **Body 1**: one central idea → explanation → specific example → link back.
3. **Body 2**: second idea (or the other view, for "discuss both") → same pattern.
4. **Conclusion** (1–2 sentences): restate position, no new ideas.

Recognise the five question types and answer *exactly* what is asked: opinion (agree/disagree), discuss both views + your opinion, advantages vs disadvantages, problem/solution (or cause/solution), two-part direct questions.

**Avoiding memorised templates**
- Memorise *functions*, not sentences: "state position", "give reason", "illustrate", "concede", "conclude".
- Ban list: "In this contemporary era", "It is a debatable topic", "This essay will discuss", "double-edged sword", "In a nutshell".
- Spend 5 minutes planning; a clear plan raises Coherence and Task Response more than fancy vocabulary.
- Examiners discount memorised chunks — they lower Lexical Resource rather than raise it.

### Speaking — target 7.5 (11–14 minutes, face-to-face)

**Part 1 (4–5 min, familiar topics)**: answer directly + reason or example, 2–3 sentences. Not one word; not a speech.

**Part 2 (1 min prep, speak up to 2 min)** — framework:
- **Prep**: write 3–4 keywords per prompt on the paper, not sentences.
- **Speak**: context (when/where/who) → the main story or details → how you felt / what happened as a result → why it matters to you. Aim to still be talking when the examiner stops you at ~2 minutes.

**Part 3 (4–5 min, abstract discussion)** — answer pattern: direct answer → reason → example (society-level, not just personal) → contrast or qualification ("That said, in rural areas…") → brief speculation about the future. Use hedging: "It largely depends on…", "I'd argue that…".

**Practising alone by recording**
1. Record a full Part 1–3 set weekly (Cambridge books, IELTS Liz topic lists).
2. Transcribe it (phone auto-transcribe).
3. Check: (a) pauses >2 s and fillers ("basically", "actually", "like"); (b) grammar range — did you use conditionals, relative clauses, passives, past perfect naturally?; (c) repeated words; (d) did every Part 3 answer include a reason and an example?; (e) pronunciation: word stress, sentence rhythm, and common South Indian English transfer features such as v/w merging and very fast, evenly-stressed delivery.
4. Re-record the same answers fixing 2 issues only.

## 6. Practice and mock test system

**How many**: 9–10 full mocks total (Week 1, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11 ×2, optional 12). More than this wastes material without time to review it.

**How to sit them**: Listening → Reading → Writing back-to-back with no breaks (that's how the real test runs). Type the Writing. Only 2 minutes of Listening review time. Speaking separately within 2 days.

### Error log (one spreadsheet, every practice session)

| Date | Book/Test | Skill & section | Q# | Question type | My answer | Correct | Error category | Root cause | Rule to fix it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 Nov | C18 T2 | L S3 | 24 | MCQ | B | C | Distractor | Picked first idea mentioned | "Wait for final agreement" |

Every Saturday, count the categories. The top 2 categories decide next week's focus.

### Progress tracker (CLB 7 → 9 → 10)

| Skill | CLB 7 | CLB 9 | CLB 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listening raw | ~23–25 | ~35–36 | ~37+ |
| Reading GT raw | ~30–31 | ~34–35 | ~37+ |
| Writing (marked) | 6.0 | 7.0 | 7.5 |
| Speaking (marked) | 6.0 | 7.0 | 7.5 |

Plot each mock. You are "at" a level only when you hit it in **two consecutive** mocks.

### Booking your test in Chennai

- **Provider**: IDP only. Book on the IDP IELTS India site or the IELTS by IDP app. Fee: **₹19,000** for General Training (same for Writing on Paper).
- **Format**: As an IT professional who types fast, choose **IELTS on Computer** — you can edit, move sentences and watch the word count. Choose Writing on Paper only if your handwriting is fast *and* your typing is error-prone without spell-check.
- **Availability**: IDP runs computer sessions up to 3 times a day, 7 days a week, so you don't need to book months ahead. Book when your Week 10–11 mocks meet the gates, for a date 2–3 weeks out. Changing a date close to the test costs a fee.
- **Slot**: choose a morning slot; check whether Speaking is the same day.
- **Results**: typically 1–5 days for computer-delivered.
- **Retake**: since One Skill Retake isn't accepted by IRCC, budget for a possible full second sitting.

## 8. Common mistakes that cap Indian test-takers

**Writing (caps at 7.0)**
1. **Indian business-email phrases in letters**: "kindly do the needful", "revert back", "prepone", "PFA", "bring to your kind notice", "Thanking you in anticipation", "Yours obediently". These read as non-standard to examiners. Use "I would be grateful if you could…", "reply", "bring forward".
2. **Tone mismatch**: formal opening with an informal close, or "I hope this letter finds you in the pink of health" to a friend.
3. **Underdeveloped bullets**: one vague sentence per bullet.
4. **Memorised openers/closers** in essays ("In this contemporary era…").
5. **Overcomplicated vocabulary used wrongly**: "plethora", "myriad", "ameliorate" in the wrong context lowers Lexical Resource. Precision beats rarity.
6. **Collocation & grammar habits**: "discuss about", "explain me", "return back", "cope up with", "do a mistake", "informations/advices/equipments/furnitures", missing articles, "I have done it yesterday".
7. **Typing errors**: IT professionals rely on autocorrect — the test has no spell-check.

**Listening (caps at 7.5)**
8. **Spelling and plurals**, not accent: one missing "s" costs a mark. The "you must use British spelling" belief is a myth — both are accepted; consistency and correctness matter.
9. **Word-limit violations** and falling for self-corrections.
10. **Losing place in Section 4** and freezing instead of jumping to the next heading.

**Reading (caps at 7.0–7.5)**
11. **Section 3 time trap**: reaching Section 3 with less than 25 minutes left; spending 8 minutes on one heading. Rule: if a question takes >90 seconds, guess, flag, move on.
12. **Using background knowledge** in TFNG, and confusing False with Not Given.

**Speaking (caps at 7.0)**
13. **Memorised Part 2 stories** forced onto the wrong cue card; examiners notice.
14. Habits: "Myself Mansoor", "I am having two years of experience", one-word Part 1 answers, speaking too fast with flat stress.

## 9. Book your test only when you can do all 10 of these

1. Score **≥ 37/40 in Listening** on two consecutive full mocks (Cambridge 20/21 GT), with only 2 minutes of review time.
2. Score **≥ 37/40 in GT Reading** on two consecutive mocks, finishing Section 3 with ≥ 2 minutes spare.
3. Get **zero errors in Reading Sections 1–2** in at least one of those mocks.
4. Write a letter **in any of the 3 tones** in ≤ 20 minutes, typed, all 3 bullets developed, with no Indian-email idioms.
5. Write a 270–300 word essay in ≤ 40 minutes for **any of the 5 question types**, without a memorised opener.
6. Receive **7.5 or higher on Writing from a human marker** at least once in Weeks 10–11 (and never below 7.0).
7. Receive **7.5 in a live Speaking mock**, with Part 2 running the full 2 minutes and every Part 3 answer containing a reason and an example.
8. Sit **L + R + W back-to-back** (2 h 45 m) without your score dropping in the last section.
9. Show an error log where **no single error category** accounts for more than 2 lost marks per test.
10. Confirm your plan covers validity: test date + 2 years comfortably covers Express Entry profile creation, a possible ITA, and PR application submission — and you've confirmed with an RCIC whether you or your wife is the principal applicant (which decides whether CLB 9 or CLB 10 is your true target).
