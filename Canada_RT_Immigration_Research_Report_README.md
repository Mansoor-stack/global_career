# Canada RT Immigration Research Report

## Overview

This document is a detailed research report on the path for a foreign-trained respiratory therapist from India to build a move, work, and immigration strategy for Canada. It is focused on a case involving Mansoor Ahmed's wife and the practical realities of healthcare immigration in Canada.

## Core topics

- licensing pathway for internationally educated respiratory therapists
- immigration options for a skilled applicant with healthcare experience
- Express Entry and healthcare-category selection details
- provincial nominee programs and province-specific opportunities
- salary expectations, wages, and job market conditions
- consultant recommendations and warnings about common scams
- official channels and free resources before paying anyone
- budget estimates and step-by-step planning roadmap

## Why this file matters

This file brings together the main decision factors that matter for success:

- licensing is a separate process from immigration and cannot be managed by a consultant
- healthcare draws require occupation-specific eligibility and timing
- licensing steps and provincial requirements vary by province
- some advisors may be misleading or unsuitable for healthcare migration
- a DIY and verification-heavy approach is often safer and more cost-effective

## Main takeaways

- The job occupation code for respiratory therapists is NOC 32103.
- The main immigration route is Express Entry, especially when healthcare-category criteria are met.
- The licensing process depends on regulator requirements, with CRTO being a major path for many applicants.
- It is generally better to start with official regulator guidance and independent verification.
- Consultant costs can become high, so a strategy consultation or file review is often preferable to full-service retention.

## Use cases

This report is best used when:

- planning the immigration timeline
- understanding licensing vs PR sequencing
- comparing provinces and pathways
- preparing to talk to consultants or advisors
- evaluating the real-world cost and complexity of the plan
